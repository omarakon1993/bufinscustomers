1. What it is for

It answers: how much cash does the business generate, how much goes to taxes, working capital and investment, how well is profit converted into cash? It is the management view of cash: it starts from operating profit and reaches net flow, passing through gross cash, working capital, investment and financing flow.

For the manager or owner
  • A strip that tells whether the business generates enough cash to sustain operations.
  • Where cash comes from and where it goes, with a waterfall from opening to closing cash.
For the accountant or financial analyst
  • The monthly detail, with a monthly or accumulated view.
  • The validation that the flow reconciles with the balance sheet cash and banks.

2. The screen

Choose the company, the scenario, the figures and the range of months. By default the range runs from January to the last executed month. Then press Generate.

  • Figures: actual (executed months only) or projected close (actual up to the last executed month and projection from there on; projected months are marked with * and look lighter).
  • Table detail: all lines or subtotals only; view monthly or accumulated; unit in pesos, thousands or millions. The unit and the view are remembered in your browser.
  • Compare with another scenario (optional).
  • Report styles: lets you choose the color palette of the report (Bufins by default; also the company's corporate colors or a custom palette). The choice is applied when you press Generate and reaches every output (screen, PDF, image, Excel and email). The company logo is shown next to its name and does not depend on the palette.

3. Where the figures come from

From the model table ModeloFlujoCaja, which is filled when the model is run in Data → Model execution. The report does not run the model: there is data only after it has been run. Unlike treasury, this table holds several years.

  • Each line carries the actual value (executed months only), the projection and the budget (the latter only for the execution year).
  • The opening cash and the validation use the “Cash and banks” line of the balance sheet (the same model as the Indicators report).

4. How it is calculated

ItemHow it is obtained
Gross cash before taxesOperating profit + depreciation, amortization and provisions.
Gross cash after taxesGross cash before taxes − income tax.
Working capital effectThe change in receivables, inventories and payables: if it consumes cash it subtracts; if it releases cash it adds.
Operating free cash flowGross cash after taxes ± working capital − investment in property, plant and equipment, intangibles and other investments.
Financing and non-operating flowFinancial income and expenses, exchange difference and other movements outside operations.
Net flowOperating free cash flow + financing flow + non-operating flow. It is the change in cash for the period.
Cash conversionOperating free cash flow ÷ operating profit. “Not applicable” if operating profit is zero or negative.
“Projected close” figuresActual value up to each year’s last executed month and projection from there on.
Opening cash“Cash and banks” from the balance sheet at the close of the month before the range.
Computed closing cashOpening cash + net flow of the range’s actual months.

When a figure has no data in the chosen measure or range, the report says so (for example, “actual” of a month not yet executed) instead of showing zeros.

5. Opening cash, closing cash and validation

The waterfall starts at opening cash, adds the sources, subtracts the uses and ends at closing cash. A strip checks that this result matches the “Cash and banks” of the balance sheet for the last actual month of the range.

  • Green strip: it reconciles, within a tolerance of a few pesos for rounding (it grows with the number of months).
  • Red strip: shows the difference, and the waterfall adds an amber “Unreconciled” bar so it closes at the balance sheet and does not hide the gap.
  • No opening cash: if the range starts at the first month that has a balance sheet, there is no previous close; the report says so and skips the waterfall (sources and uses are still shown).
  • January: in some years the model starts January with a cash balance different from the December close; a range that crosses those months can show a difference that is not a report error.

6. AI reading

  • The Generate button produces three lines: Cash generation (how much the business produces), Use of cash (where it goes) and Recommended action.
  • What is sent to the AI: only the already-calculated summary of the flow, the sources and uses and the reconciliation; no detailed accounting data and nothing from other tables is sent.
  • It counts against the company’s AI budget and respects per-user access; if AI is turned off for the company, the block does not appear.
  • The reading is a support tool: always check it against the figures on screen.

7. Export

Export button, the same as in the other reports, with the company logo and the chosen palette:

  • Excel: sheets “Summary”, “Monthly flow” (with an editable chart), “Monthly detail”, “Bridge” and “Sources, uses and cash”, with the company logo.
  • Landscape PDF: page 1 with the summary, page 2 with the charts, page 3 with cash, sources and uses, and the monthly table.
  • Copy as image and Send by email (with the Excel and PDF attached; the sending is recorded in the audit log).

8. Frequently asked questions

It says there is no data, what do I do?

Run the company’s model in Data → Model execution. If you choose “actual” and the range has months not yet executed, those months have no figure.

What does the asterisk (*) on a month mean?

That the month is projected, not actual.

The validation strip is red, what do I do?

Check that the balance sheet model and the flow model were run for the same scenario and data load. If the range crosses a January, the model may start the year with a different cash balance.

Why don’t I see the cash waterfall?

Because the range starts at the first month with a balance sheet and there is no previous close to take the opening cash from.

What is the difference between the cash flow and the statement of cash flows?

The cash flow is the management view (from operating profit to net flow); the statement of cash flows is the by-activity statement (operating, investing, financing). Both end at the same change in cash.

Can I compare against the budget?

Today you compare against another scenario; the budget is available as a figure only for the execution year.
