1. What it is for

It answers: is the budget cash enough, in which months does it fall short, how much new funding is needed, which items explain it? It takes the year’s budget (or projection) and turns it into a month-by-month cash reading.

For the manager or owner
  • A traffic-light strip: cash is enough, tight, or funding is required.
  • The month of lowest cash and how much new debt would be needed.
For the accountant or financial analyst
  • The full bridge from capital and revenue to the month’s net cash.
  • The monthly detail, with the option to see subtotals only.

2. The screen

Choose the company, the scenario, the figures and the range of months; the whole year is shown by default. Then press Generate.

  • Figures: budget or projection (the budget updated with what has already been executed).
  • Compare against the other measure (budget vs projection) and/or against another scenario.
  • Table detail: all lines or subtotals only. Unit: pesos, thousands or millions.
  • Report styles: lets you choose the color palette of the report (Bufins by default; also the company's corporate colors or a custom palette). The choice is applied when you press Generate and reaches every output (screen, PDF, image, Excel and email). The company logo is shown next to its name and does not depend on the palette.

3. Where the figures come from

From the model table ModeloTesoreriaPpto, which is filled when the treasury model is run in Data → Model execution. That model combines the income statement, the balance sheet change and the budget balance sheet. The report does not run the model: there is data only after it has been run.

  • Budget is the model’s base measure; projection is the same budget updated with what has been executed so far.
  • The formula of each subtotal lives in the model (Rel_TesoreriaPpto); the report shows them as they are.

4. How it is calculated

Cash, step by step

StepWhat is added or subtracted
1. Gross cashShare capital + current debt + revenue − cost of sales.
2. Cash before taxes− operating expenses + financial income − interest − other non-operating expenses + other non-operating income − exchange difference.
3. Net cash− income tax ± changes in property, plant and equipment, investments, working-capital net cash flow and other non-operating movements.

The indicators

ItemHow it is obtained
Accumulated cashThe month-by-month sum of net cash; it restarts each year.
Minimum balanceThe lowest value of accumulated cash in the range, with its month.
Months with negative cashHow many months in the range close with accumulated cash below zero.
New debt requiredThe range’s largest accumulated deficit: what would have to be financed so that cash never goes negative.
Status stripRed if there is an accumulated deficit (funding required); amber if cash is enough but tight; green if it is comfortably enough.

The three balance lines of the bridge (accumulated, surplus and new debt) are derived from the monthly net cash in the report itself, so they are consistent with the chosen measure and range. Values below 0.5 are treated as zero to avoid numeric noise.

5. AI reading

  • The Generate button produces three lines: Cash position (where cash ends up), Funding (whether it is needed and how much) and Recommended action.
  • What is sent to the AI: only the already-calculated summary of cash, balances and main items; no detailed accounting data and nothing from other tables is sent.
  • It counts against the company’s AI budget and respects per-user access; if AI is turned off for the company, the block does not appear.
  • The reading is a support tool: always check it against the figures on screen.

6. Export

Export button, the same as in the other reports, with the company logo and the chosen palette:

  • Excel: sheets “Summary” (indicators), “Monthly cash” (with an editable chart), “Monthly detail” and “Cash bridge”, with the company logo.
  • Landscape PDF: page 1 with the status, indicators, AI reading and key takeaways; page 2 with the charts; and the monthly table.
  • Copy as image and Send by email (with the Excel and PDF attached; the sending is recorded in the audit log).

7. Frequently asked questions

It says there is no data, what do I do?

Run the company’s treasury model in Data → Model execution and choose a scenario with data.

What is the difference between budget and projection?

The budget is the year’s target; the projection is that budget updated with what has already been executed.

What does “new debt required” mean?

It is the range’s largest accumulated deficit: the money that would have to be raised so that cash never falls below zero.

Why does accumulated cash start over in January?

Because the accumulated total restarts every year.

Can I see previous years?

No: the treasury model only stores the execution year.

Can I compare against another scenario?

Yes, with the same measure (budget or projection). The option is disabled if the other scenario has no data.
